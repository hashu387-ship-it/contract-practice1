### What is a Contract

Left column:
- A legally binding agreement with specific terms, that are agreed between two or more parties which has promise on performing certain task and against receiving a benefit for performing the task.
- A contract can be either written or oral (**exception** : contracts concerning property transactions **shall** be in writing)
- **Terms of the Contract** – terms forming part of a contract can be either in a form of;
  - **Express terms** – T&C that are specifically agreed upon by the parties
  - **Incorporated terms** – T&C that are by notice or by cause dealings (LC Payments etc)
  - **Implied terms** – terms which are not express, but are assumed to be enforceable under the law.(decennial liability)

Right column:
- **Prerequisites to form a Contract**
  - Offer
  - Acceptance
  - Consideration
  - Intention to create legal relations
  - Legal capacity
  - Legality
  - Good faith and fair deal

(Slide number: 07)

### Prerequisites to form a Contract

- **Offer** – expression of the willingness enter into a contract detailing what would be provided. Can be either written, oral or by conduct. (Person making the offer – **offeror**, person receiving the offer – **offeree)**
- **Acceptance** – Unconditional acceptance by the offeree to the offeror. If the acceptance is **conditional** then it becomes a **counter offer**, which the **offeror need accepting** to form a contract. Silent by the offeree is not generally considered as acceptance.
- **Consideration** – the benefit (monitory or any other) to be provided by the offeree in return to the offeror performing is offer.
- **Intention to create legal relations** – the parties must intend to enter into a legally binding arrangement in which the rights and obligations of the agreement are enforceable.
- **Legal capacity** – Capacity of the parties that are entering into a contract (Minors incapacitated and mentally cannot enter into a contract)
- **Legality** – The contract should be on performing a legally acceptable work/promise.
- **Good faith and fair deal** - parties to a contract will deal with each other honestly, fairly, and in good faith

### General Provisions vs Mandatory Provisions

- The Parties cannot agree to vary mandatory provisions
- **Example :**
- The parties are restricted from incorporating provisions in the contract that grant exclusive termination rights to one party (contrary to mandatory legal requirements).
- However, the parties have the flexibility to agree on restricting the Defects Liability Period (DLP) to one year, even though the law states a default DLP of three years, unless otherwise agreed by the parties.
- **Article 31 (Mandatory Provisions)**
  - A mandatory provision (of law) shall take precedence over contractual stipulation
- **Article 218 (Termination of Contracts)**
  - A contract shall not binding on one or both of the contracting parties despite its validity and effectiveness if there is a condition that such party may cancel it without mutual consent or another of the court.
- **Article 296 (Liability for Harmful Acts / Breach of Contract)**
  - Any condition purporting to provide exemption from liability for a harmful act shall be void
- **Article 882 (Decennial liability)**
  - Any agreement the purport of which is to exempt the contractor or the architect from liability (decennial) or to limit such liability, shall be void.

### Letter of Intent (LOI)

- **LOI –** Letter of Intent (general principle)
- Expression of a party's <u>**interest**</u> to enter into a contract in a **future date**
  - Does not form any contractual relationship
  - Liabilities are limited
  - One sided assurance to form a contract.
  - However, based on the provisions provided in the LOI, it may constitute a LOA.
  - If works executed under LOI, <u>**quantum merit**</u> principle shall apply
  - **Instances of using LOI –** any delay in finalizing the LOA/Contract, to engage the contractor for authority approvals, resource planning and the like.
- **Types of LOI – Generally 3**
  - **Letter of comfort** - States a party's intention for future actions but doesn't create a binding contract.
  - **Instructions to proceed with consent to spend -** It enables work to start up to a <u>**specified value**</u> while the contract is still being finalized
  - **Letters recognizing the existence of a binding contract(s)** - is used to formally finalize the contract after substantial agreement, marking the end of negotiations.

Right column:
- **Assessing the works executed based on LOI**
- **Quantum Merit** - as much as he has earned / as much as one deserve for performing the work / payment of fair & reasonable amount.
- Article 888/889 (Quantum Merit) – If the price for the work is not specified in the contract a fair remuneration together with value of materials provided should be given
- **Unjust enrichment** – One party is enriched at the expense of the other.

### Privity of Contract.

- a common law principle,
- states that only the parties involved in a contract have the right to enforce its terms or seek damages for any breaches.
- In some countries such as in KSA and Qatar, this principle may not apply.
- **Mechanism to overcome the Privity of Contract**
  - Assignment
    - ➢ Collateral Warranties
  - Novation

Right column:
- **Assignment**
- Transfers the <u>**benefit (right)**</u> of a contract from one party to another, but only the benefit, not the burden.
- Example:- Right for payment, right to take legal actions, assignment of collateral warranties.
- <u>**Collateral Warranty –**</u> *i.e. Providing certain rights to a third party (Employer, Contractor, tenant , end users)*
- They provide for a duty of care to be extended by <u>**one of the contracting parties**</u> to a <u>**third party**</u> who is not party to the original contract.
- **Novation**
- Transfers ('Novates') both the <u>**benefit and the burden**</u> of a contract from one party of the original contract to a new party, where the original party replaces with a new party. A novation creates a new contractual relationship – a 'new' contract is entered into.

### Novation

- **Novation - Two types**
- **Ab initio Novation** – An ab initio novation agreement, under which the new party who replaced the old party assumes all historic rights and liabilities.
  - **Example :-** The consultant becomes liable to the contractor for design services it carried out for the employer prior to the novation. The contractor becomes liable to the employer for all the design of the works (both pre and post novation).
- **Switch Novation –** An switch novation agreement, under which the new party who replaced the old party assumes rights and liabilities from the point in time of the novation.
  - **Example :-** the consultant is responsible to the employer for pre-novation design services and the contractor is for responsible for post novation design services.

### Performance Bond / Security / Guarantee

**Vesting Certificate.**
- Vesting certificates ensure that **materials in third-party agreements** are not retained **under the original supplier's title.**

### Extension of Time EoT

- **Why EOT –** Contractor to avoid exposing to the liability of LD / To avoid Time at Large situation
- **Time at Large –** Situation when there **is no specific completion date** or **no provision to extend time for completion in the Contract** when Employer delays the project.
- **Prevention Principle –** legal principle which holds that a party to a contract cannot insist on the performance of an obligation by the other party if it has itself prevented or hindered that performance.

  Key aspects
  1. If the Employer causes delays or disruptions, preventing the Contractor from completing the work, the **Employer cannot enforce the Time for Completion**.
  2. If Time is at large, the Constrictor [Contractor] should complete the works **within a reasonable time.**
  3. Employer **cannot apply liquidated damages** to the Contractor **if the Employer caused the delay**.

  Case law ref. *Peak Construction (Liverpool) Ltd v McKinney Foundations Ltd [1970]*

### Provisional Sums — Defined and Undefined

(Slide 44)

**Left column:**
- Definition – FIDIC 1999 (Red Book)
- "Provisional Sum" means a sum (if any) which is specified in the Contract as a provisional sum, for the execution of any part of the Works or for the supply of Plant, Materials or services under Sub-Clause 13.5 [Provisional Sums]
- Provisional sums are generally an allowance or estimate included within the contract price of a construction contract for works that are:
  1. not sufficiently defined, designed or detailed to allow an accurate determination of its cost at the time the contract is entered; and/or
  2. work that the Employer may or may not wish to be carried out.

**Right column:**
- New Measurement Rules (NRM) categorizes provisional sums as being either "defined" or "undefined".
- Defined provisional sums are those that are sufficiently well defined and/or detailed to allow the contractor to make allowances for them in their programming, planning and pricing preliminaries.
  - a description of the nature and construction of the work;
  - a statement of how and where the work is to be fixed to the building and what other work is to be fixed thereto;
  - a quantity or quantities that indicate the scope and extent of the work; and
  - any specific limitations and the like.
- Undefined provisional sums are those that fall outside of the above parameters.
- Eg:-, work required below an existing structure (i.e. piling works) where the grounds conditions, and therefore the extent of the works, cannot be determined until the existing structure is demolished and the ground opened.

  - The Contractor must give notice and consider the suspension as an omission under Clause 13 [Variations and Adjustments] for the affected part of the Work

**Right column — Suspension by the Contractor:**
- Sub-Clause 16.1 [Contractor's Entitlement to Suspension of Work]:
- The Contractor may suspend the works after giving notice to the Employer not less than 21 days, if
  - The Engineer does not certify interim payment certificates as per contract requirements (sub cl 14.6).
  - The Employer does not provide reasonable evidence of financial arrangements to pay the contract price within 28 days of the Contractor's request (sub cl 2.4).
  - The Employer fails to make payments to the Contractor in accordance with the contract terms (e.g., not paying within amount certified within 56 days after receiving the statement) (sub cl.14.7)

### Termination (Employer)

(Slide 48)

- Termination of the Contract by the Employer (clause 15.2, FIDIC 1999)
- In case of the Contractor's failure to fulfill any obligation under the Contract, the Employer is empowered by Clause 15.1 [Notice to Correct] to issue a Notice to the Contractor, requiring them to rectify the failure within a specified timeframe.
  - a) Failure to comply with Sub-Clause 4.2 [Performance Security] or a notice under Sub-Clause 15.1 [Notice to Correct].
  - b) Abandonment of the Works or clear intention not to continue fulfilling contractual obligations.
  - c) Failure to proceed with the Works without reasonable excuse as per Clause 8 [Commencement, Delays, and Suspension] and Clause 7.6 [Remedial Works]
  - d) Subcontracting the entire Works or assigning the Contract without required agreement.
  - e) Becoming bankrupt or insolvent, going into liquidation, receiving a receiving or administration order, or any similar event under applicable Laws.
  - f) Offering bribes, gifts, or inducements to influence actions related to the Contract.
- Termination procedure
  - the Employer has the right to terminate the Contract by providing the Contractor with a 14-day notice.
  - For Sub-Paragraphs (e) or (f), the Contract is terminated immediately upon the Notice issued by the Employer.

### Termination (Contractor)

(Slide 49)

- Termination of the Contract by the Contractor (clause 16.2, FIDIC 1999)
- The Contractor has the right to terminate the contract by providing the Employer with not less than 14 days' notice, except in cases of prolonged suspension or insolvency, where termination can be immediate.
  - a) There is a prolonged suspension of the Works, typically lasting for a period exceeding 84 days.
  - a) The Contractor does not receive reasonable evidence of the Employer's financial arrangements within 42 days of suspending the Works for the same reason as mentioned earlier.
  - b) The Engineer fails to issue an interim payment certificate within 56 days after receiving all the required information under the contract. (28 days + 28 days)
  - c) The Contractor is not paid the amount properly due in an interim payment certificate within 42 days after the initial 56 days for payment. (56 days + 42 days = 98 days)
  - d) The Employer substantially fails to fulfill its obligations under the contract.

### Completion, Final Account and Liquidated (& Ascertained) Damages

(Slide 51)

**Left column:**
- Final Account and Completion - Clause 14.11 [Application for Final Payment Certificate]
  - Upon 56 days after receiving the performance certificate
  - Contractor to submit draft final statement (account) with supporting documents which includes;
  - Value of all work done in accordance with the Contract, and
  - Any further sums Contractor considers due to him under Contract or otherwise
  - If agreed, final statement along with the discharge letter (sub cl. 14.12)
  - Engineer to issue final Payment certificate within 28 days after receiving the final statement + discharge letter.
  - If dispute exists, Engineer to issue a Payment certificate for the undisputed part. Disputed part referred to DAB 20.4
  - Payments to be made by the Employer within 56 days after the Employer receives the PC. (sub.cl 14.7 (c))

**Right column:**
- Liquidated & Ascertained Damages (LAD)
- LAD – Liquidated (represented by a monetary value), Ascertained (amount stared prior commencement of the contract)
- Liquidated vs Unliquidated damages
- Liquidated damages
  - Genuine pre-estimate of the Employer's potential loss due to Contractor's breach of contract.
- Unliquidated damages
  - Refer to damages that are not pre-determined or specified in the contract.
  - Assessed and determined based on the actual losses suffered by the Employer
- Q : What's the difference between Liquidated Damages and Liquidated & Ascertained Damages (LAD)
  - Liquidated damages (pre-agreed but not yet quantified).
  - Ascertained damages (pre-agreed and clearly calculable).
